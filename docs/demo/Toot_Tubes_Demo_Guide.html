1. What the app is (30-second pitch)

Say thisToot Tubes is a funny physics party game for Android. Up to four cartoon animals sit on one swim ring and push it through sky waterpark courses by farting. You aim with your left thumb and hold to fart with your right. There are five levels that unlock one by one, you can play offline in airplane mode, or with friends on phones on the same Wi-Fi, in Co-op, Versus or Teams 2v2, collecting stars.
  • Goal of each level: get the ring to the finish before the timer runs out.
  • Win: cross the finish in time → 1–3 stars by time left. Lose: timer hits 0 → Retry.
  • Star Scramble: 12 gold stars float on each course. The rider whose seat passes closest to a star takes it. In Versus or Teams, most stars wins.
  • Engine: Godot 4.7.2 (GDScript), Android, portrait, touch controls.

6. How it works (simple technical overview)

The game loop

  • The ring is one physics body. Each rider sits at a seat on its rim.
  • When you hold, your rider "farts" and pushes the ring away from your seat. All riders' pushes add up, so teamwork (or fighting) decides where the ring goes.
  • Each rider has a gas meter: it drains while farting and refills while resting. Beans refill it.
  • Falling into a hole or off the edge isn't a loss: the crew restarts at the last checkpoint while the clock keeps running.

Stars (Star Scramble)

  • 12 stars per course, placed by a formula along the course (left, right, middle…), so every phone builds the same stars with no random numbers.
  • Each frame the host checks: which occupied seat is nearest to a star and within reach (about 1.9 m)? That rider gets it, once.
  • Co-op adds everyone's stars; Versus compares riders; Teams adds seats 1+3 vs 2+4.

Saving and unlocking

  • Saved with Godot's ConfigFile to user://save.cfg on the phone: best stars + best time per level, settings, avatars.
  • Clearing level n unlocks n+1. Save happens before the result screen, so closing the app right after a win doesn't lose it.
  • Online results don't change offline unlocks (allowed by the PDF).

7. The connection (why it works on Wi-Fi and hotspot)

1. Host a game – Phone A opens a game server on port 7777 (Godot ENet, which runs on UDP).
2. "I'm here!" – once a second, Phone A shouts a tiny message to the whole local network (UDP broadcast on port 7778) with its name.
3. Games nearby – Phone B listens on 7778, sees the message and shows "Join Player's game". Typing the IP is the backup.
4. Join – Phone B connects to A's IP on 7777. If nothing answers in 5 s → clear error message.
5. Play – B sends only its own aim + hold. A runs the physics, awards stars and sends the ring position 20 times per second to everyone.
6. Result – A decides finish/time-out and the winner, and sends one result to all phones.

Why the hotspot works without internet

A phone hotspot is a small local network, just like home Wi-Fi. Both phones get local addresses (like 192.168.x.x) on it. The game never goes to the internet; it only talks phone-to-phone inside that local network. So the hotspot doesn't even need mobile data. Broadcast messages also stay inside that local network, which is why "Games nearby" shows up there too.

Why the host decides (and guests can't cheat)

  • Only one phone runs the real physics, so all phones always agree on where the ring is and who got which star.
  • Guests can't send "I won" or "I got a star". The host checks each message: only your own seat, not too often, not too strong.
  • Small lag is hidden by smoothing (interpolation) on guests. If a guest's input stops arriving, it expires so the ring doesn't keep pushing.
  • Important messages (start, ready, result) are sent reliably; the ring updates are sent fast because a new one arrives every 50 ms anyway.

If something goes wrong

  • Guest leaves → their seat becomes empty, the match continues.
  • Host leaves → guests see "Host disconnected. Your offline progress is safe." and go back to the play menu.
  • Not on the same network → nothing shows in Games nearby; Join by IP gives the error after 5 s.

How the animal riders were made

  • No downloaded models. Every animal (hippo, pig, panda, penguin, bunny, cat) is built from code.
  • The body is a lathed shape: draw a side outline (a pear shape) and spin it around a vertical line, like a potter's wheel. Ears, snouts, tails and arms are smaller spun or stretched shapes.
  • A toon shader gives the flat cartoon shading and outline-like rim light. It also adds wiggle, tail wag and butt glow when farting.
  • The fart cloud texture is drawn pixel by pixel in code.

How the environment was made

  • Each course is a curved path (a spline). The code sweeps a water channel and cliff walls along it, like squeezing toothpaste along a line.
  • The water is a shader: waves, foam and sparkles come from sine-wave formulas, not images.
  • Hazards (mallets, crushers, boulders, whirlpools) are simple shapes (boxes, cylinders, spheres) that move with code.
  • Clouds, balloons, flags and islands are also generated in code. Sound and music were synthesized by a Python script.

9. Cheat sheet: possible questions

Why farts and one shared ring?

It's a funny, simple control (aim + hold) that everyone gets in seconds. Sharing one ring forces cooperation: your push affects everyone. Stars add competition inside the same ring.

Is it co-op or competitive?

Both. The PDF allows "race, compete, or cooperate". Co-op shares the star total; Versus is every rider for themselves; Teams 2v2 is seats 1+3 vs 2+4. The host picks the mode.

How many players?

Offline: you plus up to 3 computer assistants on one phone. Online: 2–4 phones, one rider each.

How are the 1–3 level stars calculated?

Finish with more than 50% of the time left = 3 stars, more than 25% = 2, otherwise 1. Timer reaches 0 = lose.

How do you know who gets a gold star?

The host checks every frame: the nearest rider whose seat is within reach of the star gets it, once. A tie goes to the lower seat number. Guests just display what the host says.

Where is progress saved? What if I close the app?

In user://save.cfg on the phone using Godot's ConfigFile. It saves before the result screen appears, so force-closing doesn't lose the unlock.

Why does the host decide the winner?

So there's one truth. If each phone decided, they could disagree (lag) or a modified phone could cheat. Guests only send their aim and hold; the host validates them and sends the result.

How does the other phone find the game without typing an IP?

The host broadcasts a small "here I am" message once a second on UDP port 7778. Other phones listen and list it under Games nearby. Typing the IP still works as a backup.

Why did it work on a hotspot with no internet?

A hotspot is a local network. Both phones get local IPs and talk directly. The game never needs the internet.

What if the Wi-Fi is off or the host is unreachable?

After about 5 seconds: "Could not reach the host. Check its IP, Wi-Fi and port 7777." No crash, you stay on the menu.

What happens if someone disconnects mid-match?

A guest leaving → their seat becomes empty and the match goes on. The host leaving → guests get "Host disconnected. Your offline progress is safe." and return to the menu.

How do you handle lag?

The host sends the ring state 20 times a second; guests smooth between updates. Inputs expire if they stop arriving, so a rider can't get stuck pushing.

Why LAN (Option A) and not an online server (Nakama)?

Option A is required for all teams. It needs no server, no account and no internet, and it works on any Wi-Fi or hotspot. Option B (Nakama) is optional for extra credit and was not done.

Why the Compatibility renderer?

The lab requires it. It uses OpenGL ES 3, which runs on low-cost Android phones like the ones tested.

Where did the art and sounds come from?

All original, made in code: animals from lathed shapes with a toon shader, courses swept along curves, water from a shader, sounds synthesized in Python. The only outside asset is the Fredoka font (SIL OFL). Credits are in the app and in docs/CREDITS.md.

Did you use AI?

Yes, an AI coding assistant helped with parts of the code and documentation. I reviewed, tested and edited everything, and it's disclosed in the paper's acknowledgment.

How did you test it?

132 automated tests (GUT), a network test that runs real host and guest game processes (6 scenarios, including Versus and Teams), and a phone test matrix on two phones: Infinix X6525 and TECNO CLA5.

How smooth is it?

About 56–69 FPS on the Infinix and around 60 FPS on the TECNO during online play. Low quality mode is there for slower phones.

What are the ports and the package name?

Game: port 7777 (ENet/UDP). Discovery: port 7778 (UDP broadcast). Package: com.itsd85.cazo.toottubes.

Why can't the computer assistants beat me in Versus?

They push the ring with the same rules but don't hunt stars on purpose, so they're a light opponent. Versus is best with two real players.

Does online play unlock levels?

No. Online results stay separate from offline unlocks, which the PDF allows. Unlocks come from the offline campaign.
